# Lapses & exploration

<p class="meta">Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, 2014–2019 &middot; PhD with Anne Churchland</p>

Perceptual decision-makers make errors even on the easiest trials. These "lapses" are usually treated as noise and fit away with a nuisance parameter. In a multisensory rate-discrimination task in rats, we showed that lapses instead reflect a strategic trade-off between exploiting known rewarding actions and exploring uncertain ones: changing the reward magnitude or probability of one action changed only the lapses associated with that action, as the model predicted, and inactivating posterior striatum or secondary motor cortex changed lapses in a modality-specific way. Lapses carry information about action values, and about the animal's uncertainty, that the standard models throw away. (Press: [In mapping mistakes, a window to the inner mind](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/2022/05/26/in-mapping-mistakes-a-window-to-the-inner-mind), Simons Foundation.)
